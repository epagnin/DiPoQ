\subsection{Definition of lattices}
The fundamental quantity in lattice-based cryptography is a lattice. For the purposes of this text, the following definition is sufficient. 
\begin{definition}[Lattice]
    A lattice $\LL$ is a discrete additive subgroup of $\RR^\nd$.
\end{definition} 
A lattice is finitely generated by a set of linearly independent basis vectors $\{ b_1, \ldots, b_k \}$, where each $b_i \in \RR^k$, often collected in a basis matrix $B$ such that
\[ \LL = \LL(B) = B \cdot \ZZ^k = \left\{ \sum_{i = 1}^{k} z_i b_i : z_i \in \ZZ\right\}. \]
The number of linearly independent basis vectors $k$ is the \textit{rank} of the lattice. We are only intested in lattices of full rank, where $k = \nd$. A characteristic property of a given lattice $\LL$ is it's first minimum, which relates to the geometry of the lattice. This means the lattice must be endowed with a norm $|| \cdot ||$. Unless otherwise specified, we work in the Euclidean norm.
\begin{definition}[First minimum]
    The first minimum (also known as minimum distance, or simply the minimum) of a lattice is the length of the shortest nonzero lattice vector 
    \[ \lambda_1(\LL) = \min_{v \in \LL \setminus \{ 0 \} } || v ||. \]
\end{definition}
Every lattice $\LL$ induces a dual lattice defined as follows.
\begin{definition}[Dual lattice]
    The dual $\LL^*$ of a lattice $\LL$ is defined as
    \[ \LL^* = \{ u : \langle u, \LL \rangle \subseteq \ZZ \}. \]
    In words, the dual $\LL^*$ is the set of points having integer inner product with vectors in $\LL$. 
\end{definition}
The dual $\LL^*$ is also a lattice. If $B$ is a basis $\LL$, when $(B^T)^{-1}$ is a basis of $\LL^*$ ($T$ denotes transpose).
